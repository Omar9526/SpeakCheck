# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Fast, concrete feedback.** A score for each word and each sound seconds after reading is a strong "time to result" lever. The buyer sees progress in the first session, not the first month.
- **Low effort to start.** Guest mode, no registration, and practice on the buyer's own text (their IELTS essay, interview answers, slide notes) ask very little of them. Practising on material the buyer will actually say is the most valuable part of the stack.
- **Explanations in Russian** take away the barrier that English-only tools leave for this buyer.
- **A segment with urgency inside the market.** IELTS candidates, people with an interview coming up and people about to relocate have a deadline, and deadlines make people pay.
- **The product is built.** There is something to put in front of buyers now.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market may be weak on money.** "Russian-speaking adults in the CIS, starting with Kyrgyzstan" is broad, and how much these buyers will pay for a subscription is unknown. If the pain is real but the wallet is thin, the best offer still fails. Market size and willingness to pay are open questions.
2. **A commodity offer with no proof.** The scoring comes from a third-party API that anyone can call. "A score plus an explanation" can be copied, and buyers will compare on price. Nothing yet shows that using the app leads to the dream outcome (a higher IELTS speaking band, a passed interview, confident calls). Without proof, perceived likelihood is low and Pro becomes a "maybe later".
3. **Unknown cost for each session in a free-with-limits model.** Every practice costs money for Azure and DeepSeek. If the cost per session is high compared with what buyers pay, or few free users convert to paid, growing the free sharing loop loses money faster. Cost per session, conversion and retention are all unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one segment has the sharpest pain and the most money: IELTS candidates, IT workers or people relocating? Where exactly do you reach them?
- What is the dream outcome in the buyer's words, and how will you prove the app delivers it (before/after scores, band changes, testimonials)?
- What does one practice session cost you, and how many free sessions can you afford for each paying user?
- What would make someone feel stupid saying no? Could you charge double for an "IELTS Speaking in 30 days" programme rather than "unlimited practice"?
- What guarantee can you afford, for example "your score improves by X in Y weeks or your money back"?
- Is there real urgency (an exam date, an interview date) to build the offer around?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Choose one beachhead segment with a deadline (IELTS is the obvious candidate) and name the offer after that outcome, not after the features.
2. Measure the cost per session and set the free limit and the price so the paid tier covers the free users.
3. Get proof before scaling: a small group of real users with before/after score data, and a pre-sale or paid test at a real price.
4. Build the stack around the outcome: a programme, progress tracking towards the target, and a guarantee you can afford.
