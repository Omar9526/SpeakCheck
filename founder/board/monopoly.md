# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A small market that is actually small and specific.** The target is Russian-speaking adults in Kyrgyzstan who read English but freeze when they speak it. That is the kind of narrow starting market this lens likes: small enough to own first, and the next step is clear (the rest of the CIS, then other language pairs).
- **A real wedge inside the category.** Practising on *your own* text (your CV, slides, IELTS essay, a photo of a page), with each sound scored and the mistakes explained in Russian, is a sharper promise than generic courses with fixed lessons.
- **Distribution is designed in from the start.** The result card, the "challenge a friend" link and the streak work as built-in channels. Most solo products have none of this.
- **It already works.** The product is built, it has tests and it has had a security review, so the next risk is the market, not engineering.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No proprietary technology.** The core, the scoring and the explanations, is rented from Azure and DeepSeek. Any competitor can call the same APIs in a few weeks. That is an incremental improvement, not a breakthrough ten times better, and nothing so far stops a copycat by month six. Big language apps that already reach this buyer could ship the same feature.
2. **Distribution is unproven, and paid acquisition may never pay back.** The viral loop is a hope, not a measurement. With no marketing budget, cost per install unknown and willingness to pay unknown, the first 100 customers have no confirmed route to the app.
3. **A cost structure that does not get cheaper with scale.** Every session costs money twice (Azure plus the LLM), and the cost per session has not been calculated. A large free tier can burn cash faster than Pro brings it in. The suppliers set the prices, and they can raise them or sell direct.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who else sells pronunciation scoring with Russian explanations within reach of this buyer, and what do they charge? (Unknown: competitor map needed.)
- What is the secret? What do you know about Kyrgyz and CIS learners that a global app does not?
- What does one practice session cost, and how many free sessions a day can you afford?
- How exactly do the first 100 users arrive? Which communities, schools, IELTS centres or Telegram channels?
- What data or asset builds up with use (an error corpus of Russian-speaker mistakes, a library of shared texts, ties with schools) that a copycat could not buy?
- Why now? What changed that makes this possible or necessary today?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Calculate the cost per session and set a free limit the business can survive.
2. Map competitors for the Russian-speaking market. If an incumbent already does this well, narrow the niche further (for example, only IELTS speaking prep in Bishkek).
3. Show distribution in a real test: 100 users from one named channel, with the share of users who send a challenge link and the conversion of those links measured.
4. Name the moat to build over 12 months (an error dataset, B2B ties with schools or employers, a community brand) and show the first step toward it.
